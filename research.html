Research

Generative machine learning for quantum-centric supercomputing

Research Overview

I develop methods that let quantum computers and classical supercomputers work together on molecular electronic structure. Working in the group of Prof. Rahul Maitra at IIT Bombay, and currently as an exchange researcher at the RIKEN Center for Computational Science (R-CCS) in Kobe, Japan, hosted by Prof. Takahito Nakajima, I focus on quantum-centric supercomputing: the quantum processor samples the electronic configurations that matter, and classical hardware diagonalizes the Hamiltonian in that compact subspace.

The limiting factor in this approach is the samples themselves. Finite shot budgets and hardware noise miss much of the wavefunction, especially in strongly correlated molecules. My current work uses generative machine learning to learn the structure of the wavefunction from a limited number of quantum samples and expand the subspace beyond what the hardware ever measured. This builds on my earlier work on machine-learning-assisted, shallow-depth ansatz construction for variational and projective quantum eigensolvers.

First authorPreprint

Q-WAVE: Quantum Wavefunction Augmentation via Variational Autoencoders

Sonaldeep Halder, Chayan Patra, Rahul Maitra · arXiv:2609.07972 (2026)

Sample-based quantum diagonalization (SQD) loses accuracy when the important part of the wavefunction is larger than what finite-shot sampling can capture. Q-WAVE fixes this by seeding a β-annealed variational autoencoder with determinants from SqDRIFT Krylov circuits run on IBM Heron hardware, plus classical CISD determinants. The autoencoder learns where the wavefunction lives in a continuous latent space, proposes new dominant determinants beyond any fixed excitation level, and grows the subspace iteratively toward the variational ground state.

< 1 mHaerror vs. full CI across H2O and N2 dissociation
52 qubitsethylene, sub-millihartree agreement with CCSD(T)
60 qubitsCr2 stress test, chemical accuracy after PT2 correction
  • Hybrid seed. Hardware samples bring multireference character that a purely classical seed cannot reach, and CISD fills in what the samples miss. On Cr2, the combined seed was the only variant whose energy landed within chemical accuracy.
  • Compact wavefunctions. The generative loop reaches higher accuracy with far smaller diagonalization subspaces than standard SQD configuration recovery.
  • Scales polynomially. The model size grows polynomially with qubit count, independent of the full determinant space, and diagonalization only ever runs in the selected subspace.

Research Themes

Generative ML for Quantum-Centric Supercomputing

Using generative models to recover and expand the configuration space sampled by quantum hardware in sample-based quantum diagonalization. Q-WAVE uses a variational autoencoder to grow the subspace beyond the measured samples. PIGen-SQD (Quantum Science and Technology, 2026) pairs generative models with physics-informed configuration screening from perturbative measures to reconstruct accurate states from noisy hardware samples.

Generative AI for Ansatz Construction

Using Restricted Boltzmann Machines and other generative models, trained on the physics of electron correlation, to propose chemistry-inspired ansatz circuits. Rather than fixed templates, these dynamic ansatzes adapt to the molecule, predicting dominant high-rank determinants from low-rank training data.

Projective & Variational Quantum Eigensolvers

Developing the Projective Quantum Eigensolver (PQE) and VQE variants with adaptive ansatz construction for ground-state molecular energies, including zero-noise extrapolated PQE and adiabatically decoupled subsystem evolution for accuracy on noisy hardware, and supervised machine learning for dimensionality reduction.

NISQ-Era Resource Efficiency

Perturbative measures and machine learning to screen out low-impact operators before circuit execution, reducing gate count and circuit depth. This includes the COMPASS protocol for operator commutativity prescreening.

Current Directions

Strongly Correlated and Transition-Metal Systems

Extending generative subspace expansion to multireference problems such as the chromium dimer, where both classical selected-CI methods and raw hardware sampling struggle.

Benchmarking on Real Quantum Hardware

Running and validating algorithms on IBM Quantum Heron processors through Qiskit, and feeding the gap between ideal simulation and hardware performance back into algorithm design for quantum-centric supercomputing.
